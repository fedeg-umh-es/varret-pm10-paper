\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

The results are presented in the order of the decision audit: error eligibility, dynamic fidelity, discordance, and the resulting change in eligibility. Table~\ref{tab:model_summary} reports model-level medians and counts; Table~\ref{tab:decision_rule} reports the rule comparison.

\subsection{Error-based eligibility}
Rule A identifies 277 eligible cells: 111 HGB, 117 Ridge, 44 SARIMA, 5 seasonal naive, and 0 STL+Ridge. This is an eligibility count, not a claim that one model is globally best or that the cells are independent.

\subsection{Dynamic-fidelity behaviour}
The model-level summaries show different error--fidelity profiles. Variance collapse ($\alpha<0.50$) occurs in 118/119 HGB cells, 118/119 Ridge cells, and 110/119 SARIMA cells. Seasonal naive has 0/119 collapsed cells, while STL+Ridge has 0/119 collapsed cells but a median $\alpha$ above one. Figure~\ref{fig:horizon_profiles} shows the corresponding horizon-wise medians. These are descriptive patterns within the benchmark, not causal or universal properties of the model classes.

\subsection{Error--fidelity discordance}
Figure~\ref{fig:skill_vs_alpha} displays the joint diagnostic. Across all 595 cells, Spearman's correlation between $\alpha$ and skill is $\rho=-0.863$. The correlation is descriptive and does not imply a causal mechanism.

There are 101 discordant cells under the prespecified definition (Rule A, P75 recall at least 0.20, and $\alpha<0.50$): 54 HGB, 43 Ridge, and 4 SARIMA. Thus positive error skill and adequate P75 recall can coexist with low retained variance in this benchmark.

\subsection{Effect on model eligibility}
Adding the two fidelity requirements changes eligibility from 277 cells under Rule A to 8 under Rule B: HGB 111$\rightarrow$1, Ridge 117$\rightarrow$1, SARIMA 44$\rightarrow$1, seasonal naive 5$\rightarrow$5, and STL+Ridge 0$\rightarrow$0. The 269 changed cells are therefore 269/277 = 97.1\% of Rule-A eligible cells. Figure~\ref{fig:eligibility_change} presents the same comparison graphically.
