\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

The results follow the eligibility audit: error-based eligibility, dynamic fidelity, formal discordance, the primary eligibility consequence, and deterministic threshold sensitivity. Table~\ref{tab:model_summary} reports model-level medians and counts; Table~\ref{tab:decision_rule} reports the primary rule comparison.

\subsection{Error-based eligibility}
Rule A identifies 277 eligible cells: 111 HGB, 117 Ridge, 44 SARIMA, 5 seasonal naive, and 0 STL+Ridge. These are eligibility counts within the benchmark, not population rankings and not evidence that one model is the final winner for every station and horizon.

\subsection{Dynamic-fidelity behaviour}
Variance collapse ($\alpha<0.50$) occurs in 118/119 HGB cells, 118/119 Ridge cells, and 110/119 SARIMA cells. Seasonal naive has 0/119 collapsed cells, while STL+Ridge has 0/119 collapsed cells and a median alpha above one. Figure~\ref{fig:horizon_profiles} shows how the median skill and alpha profiles vary with horizon. These are descriptive patterns within the evaluated benchmark and do not establish an architectural or causal mechanism.

\subsection{Error--fidelity discordance}
Figure~\ref{fig:skill_alpha} shows the joint diagnostic. Across all 595 cells, Spearman correlation between alpha and skill is $\rho=-0.863$. This pooled association is descriptive and partly reflects differences among model-family profiles; it should not be interpreted as a homogeneous relationship within every family or horizon.

There are 101 formal discordant cells: 54 HGB, 43 Ridge, and 4 SARIMA. They satisfy Rule A, have P75 recall of at least 0.20, and have $\alpha<0.50$. Thus, within this benchmark, positive error eligibility and adequate P75 recall can coexist with low retained variance.

\subsection{Effect on model eligibility}
Adding the fidelity requirements changes eligibility from 277 cells under Rule A to 8 under Rule B: HGB 111$\rightarrow$1, Ridge 117$\rightarrow$1, SARIMA 44$\rightarrow$1, seasonal naive 5$\rightarrow$5, and STL+Ridge 0$\rightarrow$0. The 269 changed cells are 269/277 = 97.1\% of Rule-A eligible cells.

\subsection{Rule-B threshold sensitivity}
The primary result uses alpha $\geq0.50$ and P75 recall $\geq0.20$. A deterministic sensitivity audit over the neighbouring grid $\alpha\in\{0.40,0.50,0.60\}$ and recall $\in\{0.10,0.20,0.30\}$ retains the qualitative conclusion: 91.3--98.2\% of Rule-A cells change status across the tested neighbourhood, with the exact values reported in Table~\ref{tab:rule_sensitivity}. The primary point remains 8 retained cells and 97.1\% changed. This is robustness of the decision consequence within the tested operational neighbourhood, not evidence that either threshold is optimal.
