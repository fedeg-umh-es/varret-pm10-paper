\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

The results move from conventional error eligibility to the dynamic
properties of those eligible cells, then to the decision consequence of
combining both requirements. This order matters: the reduction from 277
to 8 cells is meaningful only after the two dimensions have been shown to
disagree.

\subsection{Error skill and dynamic fidelity diverge across model families}

Rule A identifies a substantial set of cells that improve on persistence:
111 HGB, 117 Ridge, 44 SARIMA, 5 seasonal-naive, and no STL+Ridge cells.
These are eligibility counts within the benchmark, not population rankings
and not evidence that one model is the final winner for every station and
horizon. Table~\ref{tab:model_summary} gives the corresponding family
medians and significance counts.

\input{tables/table2_model_summary.tex}

The families that contribute most of the error-eligible cells also occupy
the low-alpha part of the diagnostic space. Alpha is below 0.50 in 118 of
119 HGB cells, 118 of 119 Ridge cells, and 110 of 119 SARIMA cells. By
contrast, seasonal naive has no low-alpha cells, while STL+Ridge also has
none but contributes no Rule-A cells. Figure~\ref{fig:horizon_profiles}
shows how these contrasts persist or evolve from horizon 1 through 7: skill
and retained variance are not interchangeable summaries of forecast
quality. The patterns are descriptive and do not establish an
architectural or causal mechanism.

\subsection{Error--fidelity discordance appears within the error-eligible space}

The cell-level diagnostic makes the family contrast more precise. Figure~\ref{fig:skill_alpha}
places persistence-relative skill against variance retention using common
axes for the five families. The shaded area marks only positive skill with
$\alpha<0.50$; the open contours mark the stricter formal discordance
definition, which also requires Rule A and P75 recall of at least 0.20.

There are 101 formal discordant cells: 54 HGB, 43 Ridge, and 4 SARIMA.
Thus, positive error eligibility and adequate P75 recall can coexist with
low retained variance in identifiable station--model--horizon cells. The
pooled Spearman association between alpha and skill is $\rho=-0.863$, but
this is descriptive: it partly reflects the separation among model-family
profiles visible in the facets and should not be read as a homogeneous
within-family law.

\subsection{Fidelity requirements materially change model eligibility}

The joint evidence changes the decision set when fidelity is made an
explicit requirement. Rule B retains one HGB, one Ridge, one SARIMA, five
seasonal-naive, and no STL+Ridge cells, compared with 277 cells under Rule
A. The total therefore falls from 277 to 8, with 269 cells changing status;
269/277 is 97.1\% of the Rule-A eligible cells. Table~\ref{tab:decision_rule}
puts that transition at the centre of the comparison.

The natural objection is that this consequence may depend on one
operational threshold pair. The deterministic sensitivity audit addresses
that objection without refitting forecasts. Across $\alpha$ thresholds of
0.40, 0.50, and 0.60 and P75-recall thresholds of 0.10, 0.20, and 0.30,
91.3--98.2\% of Rule-A cells change status. The primary point remains 8
retained cells and 97.1\% changed. Figure~\ref{fig:rule_sensitivity} shows
the tested neighbourhood; exact retained counts are provided in
Supplementary Table S3. The result supports persistence of the qualitative
decision consequence, not threshold optimality or universal robustness.
