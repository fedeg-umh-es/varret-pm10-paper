\section{Verification Metrics and Statistical Procedures}
\label{sec:metrics}

\subsection{Error and baseline-relative skill}
For matched forecast--observation pairs, RMSE is the square root of mean squared forecast error. Persistence-relative skill is
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{Skill}=1-\frac{\mathrm{RMSE}_{model}}{\mathrm{RMSE}_{persistence}}.
\end{equation}
Positive skill indicates lower RMSE than persistence; it does not establish dynamic fidelity.

\subsection{Variance retention and event recall}
The canonical dynamic diagnostic is the predicted-to-observed variance ratio
\begin{equation}
\alpha=\frac{\operatorname{Var}(\hat y)}{\operatorname{Var}(y)},
\end{equation}
computed with population variances ($\mathrm{ddof}=0$) over matched cell pairs. It is a variance ratio, not a standard-deviation ratio. P75 recall is the proportion of observed P75 exceedances correctly identified by the forecast, with the threshold estimated from the corresponding training window.

\subsection{Diebold--Mariano testing and multiplicity}
The loss differential compares squared errors from the candidate forecast and persistence. The two-sided Diebold--Mariano statistic uses the Harvey--Leybourne--Newbold small-sample correction and horizon-dependent autocovariance truncation. Benjamini--Hochberg adjustment is applied within each dataset to the valid model--horizon p-values before the significance flag is defined at 0.05. This family definition is important because the 595 cells share data and are not treated as independent tests in a single inferential population.

\subsection{Bootstrap uncertainty}
The canonical variance diagnostic includes descriptive percentile-bootstrap intervals computed with 1,000 resamples and seed 42. These intervals describe uncertainty around cell-level alpha estimates; they are not used to optimise Rule-B thresholds and are not interpreted as population-level inference over the structured grid.
