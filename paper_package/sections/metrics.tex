\section{Verification metrics and statistical procedures}
\label{sec:metrics}

For matched forecast--observation pairs, RMSE is the square root of mean
squared forecast error. Persistence-relative skill is

\begin{equation}
\mathrm{Skill}=1-\frac{\mathrm{RMSE}_{\mathrm{model}}}
{\mathrm{RMSE}_{\mathrm{persistence}}}.
\label{eq:skill}
\end{equation}

Positive skill indicates lower RMSE than persistence. It does not, by
itself, establish preservation of dynamic behaviour.

The primary dynamic diagnostic is the predicted-to-observed variance ratio,

\begin{equation}
\alpha=\frac{\operatorname{Var}(\hat y)}{\operatorname{Var}(y)},
\label{eq:alpha}
\end{equation}

computed with population variances over matched cell pairs. Alpha is a
variance ratio, not a standard-deviation ratio. P75 recall is the
proportion of observed P75 exceedances correctly identified by the
forecast; its threshold is estimated from the corresponding training
window to respect the rolling-origin design.

The loss differential for candidate forecasts and persistence is assessed
with a two-sided Diebold--Mariano statistic using the
Harvey--Leybourne--Newbold small-sample correction and horizon-dependent
autocovariance truncation. Benjamini--Hochberg adjustment is applied to
the valid model--horizon p-values within each dataset before significance
is assigned at $p<0.05$.

The canonical alpha diagnostics include descriptive percentile-bootstrap
intervals computed from 1,000 resamples with seed 42. These intervals
describe cell-level uncertainty; they are not used to optimise Rule-B
thresholds and are not interpreted as population-level inference over the
structured grid.
