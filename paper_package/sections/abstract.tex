\begin{abstract}
Forecast evaluation commonly prioritises error reduction, but lower error does not necessarily imply preservation of the temporal dynamics that matter for air-quality decisions. We ask whether adding dynamic-fidelity criteria to conventional error-based statistical evaluation materially changes model eligibility in multi-step daily PM$_{10}$ forecasting. Using a rolling-origin benchmark of 17 Spanish monitoring stations, five model families, and seven horizons, we audit 595 station--model--horizon cells. Under Rule A (positive persistence-relative RMSE skill and significant Benjamini--Hochberg-adjusted Diebold--Mariano comparison with persistence), 277 cells are eligible. Rule B adds two operational fidelity criteria---variance retention $\alpha\geq0.50$ and 75th-percentile exceedance recall $\geq0.20$---and leaves 8 eligible cells. Thus, 269 of the 277 Rule-A cells change status (97.1\%). Across all cells, Spearman correlation between $\alpha$ and skill is $\rho=-0.863$, and 101 cells meet the prespecified discordance definition: Rule A eligibility, adequate P75 recall, and $\alpha<0.50$. These results show that error and dynamic fidelity provide distinct information for model selection in this benchmark. The thresholds are operational audit criteria, not universal standards, and the non-independent cells do not support population-level inference beyond the evaluated setting.
\end{abstract}
