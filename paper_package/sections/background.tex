\section{Evaluation Context and EMS Gap}
\label{sec:background}

Reviews of air-quality forecasting report extensive use of RMSE, MAE, correlation, and related point-error summaries when comparing statistical and machine-learning forecasts \cite{mendez2023,tang2024,chadalavada2025}. These measures are useful for assessing predictive error, but reporting of forecast variability and event sensitivity is less consistent. The distinction is especially relevant for daily pollution series, in which an average-error improvement may not describe whether elevated episodes retain their observed amplitude.

Rolling-origin validation is an established way to respect temporal order in autoregressive forecasting and reduce leakage risk \cite{tashman2000,bergmeir2018}. It does not, however, determine which diagnostics should define eligibility after the forecasts are generated. A study can be temporally careful and still use an error-only filter. The EMS corpus audit therefore focuses on a narrow decision gap: not whether variance or event metrics exist, but whether their explicit combination with error eligibility is shown to change the set of passing cells across sites, model families, and horizons.

The audited corpus contains studies that report error, dynamic, event, or statistical-comparison dimensions in different combinations. That heterogeneity supports the motivation for a transparent audit, but it is not treated as proof of absence from the wider literature. Paper A contributes the empirical decision audit in the benchmark below; it does not claim that variance retention, exceedance recall, or multi-dimensional verification are novel measurements.
