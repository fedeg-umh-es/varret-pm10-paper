\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{What changes when fidelity enters environmental model evaluation}

The eligibility analysis revealed a large separation between forecasts that
improved on persistence and forecasts that also reproduced the selected
dynamic properties of the observed PM$_{10}$ series. Rule A admitted 277
cells, but adding the stated alpha and P75-recall requirements left 8; the
101 formal discordant cells show why the change is not merely a difference
between family-level averages. Error skill and dynamic fidelity can therefore
lead to different cell-level screening outcomes under the stated rules.

The thresholds are study-specific heuristic screening conventions, and the
595 cells share stations and underlying series. The 97.1\% figure therefore
describes the proportion of cells passing Rule A that changed under Rule B in
this benchmark; it is not a population rate or an operational acceptance
rate.

RMSE remains an appropriate and necessary measure of point-forecast error.
The analysis does not reject it. Rather, it separates the question of error
magnitude from the question of whether a forecast retains the variance and
exceedance behaviour that may matter for a particular use. A forecast can
reduce many squared errors while attenuating its trajectory, while another
can retain spread but mistime or mis-size events. Treating those properties
as complementary diagnostics makes the reason for a cell's screening status
more explicit.

The model-family profiles reinforce this interpretation without identifying
a causal architectural mechanism. HGB, Ridge, and SARIMA contribute many
Rule-A cells but also show frequent low-alpha cells. Seasonal naive retains
variance in this diagnostic while contributing few error-eligible cells;
STL+Ridge avoids low alpha at the point-estimate level but has poor
persistence-relative skill and contributes no Rule-A cells. Different
evaluation dimensions consequently reward different forecast properties,
and a family that looks favourable on one dimension need not be favourable
under the joint cell-screening rule.

\subsection{Interpretation, transferability and limits}

The result sits within an established evaluation conversation rather than
introducing a new metric. Baseline-relative accuracy comparison,
predictive-accuracy testing, temporal validation, and multidimensional
forecast verification are all established practices
\cite{hyndman2006,diebold1995,harvey1997,tashman2000,bergmeir2018,gneiting2011,jolliffe2012}.
Air-quality forecasting reviews likewise show why error summaries remain a
central part of environmental model assessment
\cite{mendez2023,tang2024,chadalavada2025}. The narrower contribution here
is to quantify the cell-level screening consequence of placing explicit
dynamic-fidelity conditions after a conventional error screen across a
structured multi-station, multi-family, multi-horizon benchmark.

Because the 97.1\% result depends on study-specific cut-offs, a
moderate change in those cut-offs could in principle remove the apparent
decision consequence. It does not disappear across the evaluated 3 x 3
neighbourhood: 91.3--98.2\% of Rule-A cells change status. This supports a
robust qualitative conclusion within the tested neighbourhood, not the
optimality of alpha 0.50 or P75 recall 0.20 and not universal robustness of
Rule B.

The bounded methodological implication from this PM$_{10}$ case is that
evaluation conclusions in analogous forecasting studies may change when
baseline-relative error performance and preservation of decision-relevant
dynamics are treated as distinct diagnostic requirements. This benchmark does
not establish a universal rule, measure the value of any specific
intervention, or demonstrate operational deployment utility. The 97.1\%
change, the 277-to-8 transition, and the family-specific collapse rates remain
properties of this PM$_{10}$ case.

The analysis concerns daily
PM$_{10}$ from 17 Spanish monitoring stations, five model families, seven
horizons, deterministic point forecasts, and five expanding rolling-origin
folds. The station--model--horizon cells are structured rather than
independent, Rule-B thresholds are study-specific, and no causal mechanism
for low alpha is established. Historical SARIMA configuration records
conflict, so the exact order is not asserted and reconstruction is limited
by the recovered aggregate provenance. Transfer to other pollutants,
countries, temporal resolutions, horizons, or model families requires a
separate evaluation.
