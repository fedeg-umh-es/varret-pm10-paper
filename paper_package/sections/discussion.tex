\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{The main decision consequence}
The central result is not the introduction of alpha as a metric. It is the change in the set of eligible forecast cells when dynamic fidelity is added to an error-based screen. In this benchmark, 277 cells pass Rule A and only 8 pass Rule B, so 269 Rule-A cells fail at least one stated fidelity condition. The result makes the practical consequence of multi-dimensional verification explicit: the answer to ``does this cell improve on persistence?'' is not equivalent to the answer to ``does this cell also meet the selected dynamic-fidelity requirements?''

\subsection{Error and fidelity are complementary}
RMSE remains an appropriate and necessary summary of point-forecast error. The audit does not reject it or imply that lower RMSE is uninformative. It shows instead that RMSE skill is a different property from the variance and episode behaviour represented by alpha and P75 recall. A model can reduce the magnitude of many errors while attenuating the spread of its trajectory. Conversely, a forecast can retain spread while mistiming or mis-sizing events. Reporting these dimensions together clarifies which requirement causes a cell to pass or fail.

\subsection{Model-family profiles}
The model-family profiles illustrate several trade-offs without establishing architectural causality. HGB, Ridge, and SARIMA contribute many Rule-A cells but also show frequent low-alpha cells. Seasonal naive retains variance in this diagnostic but contributes few error-eligible cells. STL+Ridge avoids low alpha at the point-estimate level but has poor persistence-relative skill and therefore contributes no Rule-A cells. The appropriate interpretation is not that one family is universally preferable, but that different families occupy different positions in the joint eligibility space.

\subsection{Implications for environmental model evaluation}
The EMS audit does not suggest that variance diagnostics, event metrics, or multi-dimensional verification are new inventions. Existing forecast-verification work emphasises that accuracy and other properties should be interpreted jointly, and air-quality reviews document the prevalence of error-based reporting \cite{gneiting2011,jolliffe2012,mendez2023,tang2024,chadalavada2025}. The contribution of this paper is narrower: it quantifies the eligibility consequence of adding explicit fidelity conditions to an error-based rule over a structured multi-station, multi-model, multi-horizon benchmark.

The transferable methodological insight is therefore about evaluation design rather than empirical prevalence: environmental model-evaluation conclusions can change when baseline-relative error performance and preservation of decision-relevant dynamics are treated as distinct evaluation dimensions. The magnitude observed here belongs to this PM$_{10}$ benchmark and should not be transferred as a general rate for other environmental systems.

\subsection{Threshold sensitivity and operational interpretation}
The deterministic sensitivity audit shows that the large change in eligibility persists in the tested neighbourhood around alpha 0.50 and P75 recall 0.20. This supports robustness of the qualitative decision consequence, not optimality of the selected thresholds. In an operational application, preserving dynamic spread or detecting elevated episodes may matter when forecasts inform alert thresholds, episode planning, or amplitude-sensitive decisions. The benchmark does not measure the value of any specific operational intervention, so these implications remain conditional.

\subsection{Limitations and boundaries}
The evidence is bounded to daily PM$_{10}$ from 17 Spanish monitoring stations, five model families, seven horizons, and five expanding rolling-origin folds. The 595 cells share stations and underlying series and are not independent replicates. Rule-B thresholds are study-specific. The analysis uses deterministic point forecasts, establishes no causal mechanism for low alpha, and has limited transferability to other pollutants, countries, temporal resolutions, horizons, or model families. The aggregate evidence is recovered and frozen, and the row-level provenance notes a specific limitation in independently reconstructing SARIMA skill from the combined row-level release. Historical SARIMA configuration records conflict; the manuscript therefore does not assert an exact order beyond the fixed-order family description supported by the evaluation package.
