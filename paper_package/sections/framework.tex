\section{Eligibility rules and decision audit}
\label{sec:framework}

The analysis applies two post-evaluation filters to every station--model--
horizon cell. They quantify how many cells pass a conventional error
screen and how many remain when selected dynamic properties are made
explicit. The filters do not choose one final winner for each station and
horizon; they audit cell eligibility.

\subsection{Error-based eligibility: Rule A}

\begin{equation}
\textbf{Rule A:}\qquad \mathrm{Skill}>0\quad\text{and}\quad
\mathrm{DM}_{BH}\ \text{significant}.
\label{eq:rule-a}
\end{equation}

Skill is persistence-relative RMSE skill. The significance flag is based
on a two-sided Harvey--Leybourne--Newbold modified Diebold--Mariano
comparison, with Benjamini--Hochberg adjustment applied to the valid
model--horizon comparisons within each dataset. The multiplicity family is
therefore defined by the comparison structure, not by treating all 595
cells as independent tests.

\subsection{Fidelity-aware eligibility: Rule B}

\begin{equation}
\textbf{Rule B:}\qquad \text{Rule A}\quad\text{and}\quad
\alpha\geq0.50\quad\text{and}\quad \mathrm{Recall}_{P75}\geq0.20.
\label{eq:rule-b}
\end{equation}

The alpha and P75-recall thresholds are operational criteria for this
audit. They were not estimated as optimal values and are not proposed as
universal standards or regulatory recommendations. A changed cell passes
Rule A but fails at least one Rule-B condition.

Formal error--fidelity discordance is defined more narrowly than the
two-dimensional region shown in Figure~\ref{fig:skill_alpha}: a cell must
pass Rule A, have P75 recall of at least 0.20, and have $\alpha<0.50$.
This distinction is necessary because positive skill and low alpha alone
do not establish the formal discordance definition.
