\title[Descriptive horizon gains vs global inferential support in \PMten{} forecasting]
{When descriptive forecast-horizon gains do not imply global evidence
of incremental meteorological predictability: a multi-site \PMten{} study}

% Author details for submission.
\author*[1]{\fnm{Federico} \sur{García Crespí}}\email{fedeg@umh.es}
\affil*[1]{\orgdiv{Departamento de Ingeniería de Computadores},
  \orgname{Universidad Miguel Hernández de Elche},
  \orgaddress{\city{Elche}, \country{Spain}}}

\abstract{Improvements over persistence are often reported as extended forecast skill at
longer horizons, but such descriptive gains need not imply correspondingly strong
statistical support that an added information set contributes incremental predictive
signal.  We evaluate hourly \PMten{} forecasts at nine sites (Madrid Casa de Campo and
eight Irish stations) over January--July 2023 using an expanding-window rolling-origin
scheme with 24-h spacing between forecast origins and horizons $h=1,\ldots,24$.  The
incremental comparison pairs direct multi-horizon XGBoost models using pollutant lags
with the same learner augmented by meteorological observations available at the
forecast origin (an observational information-set experiment rather than the use of
future NWP forecasts).  In Madrid, meteorology yields a pronounced descriptive change
in the skill profile, increasing the strict longest-contiguous positive
persistence-relative skill horizon from 9 to 17~h (+8~h), while the relaxed
last-positive-skill horizon increases from 15 to 17~h (+2~h).  Yet in 36 dimensionally
corrected DM-HLN comparisons at $h\in\{1,6,12,24\}$, no test survives global Bonferroni
correction under either the overlap-based specification ($q_{\text{overlap}}=0$; 0/36)
or a fixed automatic Newey--West sensitivity allowing positive-lag dependence (0/36).
The evidence therefore supports a clear descriptive horizon extension at some sites,
but no site--horizon comparison survives global multiplicity control under either
audited dependence specification; only limited within-station signals remain.}

\keywords{PM10, rolling-origin evaluation, persistence-relative skill, forecast horizon, Diebold--Mariano test, multiplicity, Newey--West}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Forecast usefulness in air quality is inherently horizon-dependent: a model that
improves near-term accuracy may offer little advantage later, and the practical value of
an hourly \PMten{} forecast depends on how long it remains better than a simple
persistence reference.  Persistence is therefore more than a baseline convenience: it
sets a demanding yardstick for whether a forecasting system adds information beyond the
most recent observation, in a setting where concentrations are shaped by emissions and
meteorologically driven transport and mixing \citep{Seinfeld2006, Kumar2016}.

Reported improvements from meteorological covariates complicate interpretation because
pollutant history already reflects some of the same processes.  When meteorology is
added to a data-driven model, an observed reduction in error may represent genuinely
incremental information, or it may reflect a re-expression of structure already present
in the lagged \PMten{} signal, with the balance varying by site and by lead time.
Accordingly, performance gains documented across the literature span a wide range of
designs and outcomes \citep{Mendez2023, Pak2018, Bai2018, AnsariQuaff2025}.

This motivates an interpretive gap that is often left implicit: extending the range of
horizons over which a model outperforms persistence is descriptively meaningful, but it
does not by itself resolve how strongly that extension can be attributed to the added
meteorological information once sampling uncertainty and multiple comparisons are taken
into account.

We study this issue in a nine-site hourly \PMten{} experiment (Madrid Casa de Campo and
an eight-station Irish network) evaluated over January--July 2023 with an
expanding-window rolling-origin design.  Persistence and SARIMA provide reference
benchmarks, and the incremental comparison pairs direct multi-horizon XGBoost models
based on pollutant lags with the same learner augmented by meteorological observations
available at the forecast origin.  Horizon-wise performance is summarised
descriptively through persistence-relative RMSE skill curves and horizon descriptors
$H^*$, while inferential attribution is assessed with DM-HLN tests \citep{Diebold1995,
Harvey1997} at $h\in\{1,6,12,24\}$ under global multiplicity control across the 36
planned site--horizon comparisons.

Against this design, the results show that sizeable horizon extensions can arise in a
way that looks compelling descriptively, yet provide weak family-wise global evidence
for incremental meteorological predictability once the planned inferential family is
accounted for.  At the same time, the family-wise conclusion is unchanged when residual
positive-lag dependence is allowed through a fixed automatic Newey--West sensitivity,
clarifying which aspects of the evidence are global and stable and which remain local
and specification-sensitive.

\section{Data}
\label{sec:data}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\subsection{Madrid}

Hourly \PMten{} concentrations and concurrent meteorological measurements were
obtained for Madrid Casa de Campo from the Madrid City Council open-data portal.
The station is classified as an urban-background site on the western edge of
the city, away from major point sources, and is representative of citywide
background conditions.  The study period spans January 2020 through July 2023;
records with missing \PMten{} or meteorological data were flagged and excluded
from model training and evaluation on a per-origin basis.  Seven meteorological
variables were retained: precipitation (\textit{rain}), temperature
(\textit{temp}), relative humidity (\textit{rhum}), mean sea-level pressure
(\textit{msl}), wind speed (\textit{wdsp}), wind direction (\textit{wddir}),
and solar radiation.

\subsection{Ireland}

Hourly \PMten{} concentrations and meteorological covariates were obtained from
the Irish Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) monitoring network.  Eight
stations distributed across Ireland were selected on the basis of data
completeness over the study window (January 2020 -- July 2023):
Birr (Co.\ Offaly), Dublin Airport, Dundalk (Co.\ Louth), Pearse Street
Dublin, Ringsend Dublin, Edenderry (Co.\ Offaly), Henry Street Limerick, and
Portlaoise (Co.\ Laois).  Meteorological variables were extracted from the nearest
mapped EPA synoptic station for each monitoring site.

Table~\ref{tab:descriptive} summarises the basic \PMten{} statistics for all
nine sites over the training and evaluation periods.  Concentrations are
generally low to moderate, with training-period means ranging from
11.3~\si{\micro\gram\per\cubic\metre} (Portlaoise) to
19.1~\si{\micro\gram\per\cubic\metre} (Dublin Airport), and Madrid at
18.4~\si{\micro\gram\per\cubic\metre}.  The 95th percentile values indicate
that episode concentrations above 40~\si{\micro\gram\per\cubic\metre} occur
at most stations, underscoring the relevance of medium-horizon forecasting.
Data completeness is high: missing rates are below 2\% at all
sites during both periods.

\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\subsection{Backtesting protocol}

We adopt a rolling-origin expanding-window backtesting scheme to preserve
prospective temporal information flow and strict separation between training
and evaluation data.  For each forecast origin $t$, the training window covers
all available observations up to (but not including) $t$; no future information
is used at any point.  The forecast origin advances in daily strides of 24~h,
producing one $h = 1, \ldots, 24$ forecast vector per day.

The evaluation period was 1 January--31 July 2023, with forecast origins spaced
by 24~h.  The number of valid evaluated origins varied by site according to data
availability.
The mandatory minimum training window is 8\,760 observations (one year), so
origins in early 2023 draw on three years of history.  This protocol prevents
any form of look-ahead bias.  Model configurations and feature sets used in the
evaluation were held fixed across evaluation origins; no origin-level tuning or
cross-validation was performed.

\subsection{Model specifications}

\subsubsection{Persistence.}
The persistence model predicts $\hat{y}_{t+h} = y_t$ for all $h$.  It serves
as the denominator benchmark for the skill score.

\paragraph{SARIMA.}
We fit a SARIMA(1,0,1)(1,0,0)$_{24}$ model at each origin using the
\textit{statsmodels} implementation of exact maximum likelihood.  To avoid
numerical instability of the Kalman filter on very long training windows, the
training series is truncated to the most recent 17\,520 observations
(approximately two calendar years) at each origin.  Multi-step-ahead forecasts
are generated by iterating the fitted state-space model forward.  SARIMA is
treated as a univariate statistical reference; it does not receive
meteorological inputs.

\paragraph{XGBoost --- lags only.}
A separate XGBoost regressor \citep{Chen2016} is trained for each horizon
$h \in \{1, \ldots, 24\}$ (the \emph{direct} multi-horizon strategy).  The
feature set consists of eight lagged \PMten{} values
($y_{t-1}, y_{t-2}, y_{t-3}, y_{t-6}, y_{t-12}, y_{t-24}, y_{t-48},
y_{t-168}$) and four calendar indicators (hour of day, day of week, month,
Julian day).  Hyperparameters are fixed across all origins and horizons
(Table~\ref{tab:xgb_params}) to ensure fair comparison; no origin-level
cross-validation is performed.

\paragraph{XGBoost --- lags + meteorology.}
Identical to the lags-only model except that the meteorological variables
measured at time $t$ are appended to the feature vector (seven for Madrid, nine for the Irish stations).  This is an
\emph{observational} setup in which meteorological information is available at
the moment of forecast issuance.  Madrid uses concurrent station observations,
whereas the Irish covariates come from the nearest mapped EPA synoptic station.
Because observed meteorological values are used
rather than numerical weather prediction (NWP) forecasts, the reported skill
scores should not be interpreted as operational performance with NWP inputs (see Section~\ref{sec:discussion}).

\subsection{Evaluation metrics}

\subsubsection{Skill score.}
For each model $m$ and horizon $h$, the RMSE skill score relative to
persistence is:
\begin{equation}
  S_m(h) = 1 - \frac{\mathrm{RMSE}_m(h)}{\mathrm{RMSE}_{\text{pers}}(h)},
  \label{eq:skill}
\end{equation}
where the RMSEs are computed over all forecast origins in the evaluation period.
Positive $S_m(h)$ indicates that model $m$ outperforms persistence at horizon
$h$.

\paragraph{Useful forecast horizon \texorpdfstring{$H^*$}{H*}.}
We report two descriptive persistence-relative horizon summaries based on the sign of
$S_m(h)$ over $h\in\{1,\ldots,24\}$:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{$H^*_{\text{strict}}$ (primary)}: the length of the longest \emph{contiguous} run of horizons with $S_m(h)>0$.
  \item \textbf{$H^*_{\text{relax}}$ (secondary)}: the last horizon $h$ for which $S_m(h)>0$.
\end{itemize}
$H^*$ is a descriptor of the skill-curve sign pattern and is not itself a hypothesis
test.

\paragraph{Diebold--Mariano test (DM-HLN), dependence specification, and multiplicity.}
Incremental meteorological information is assessed inferentially via horizon-wise
Diebold--Mariano tests \citep{Diebold1995} with the Harvey--Leybourne--Newbold
finite-sample correction \citep{Harvey1997} (DM-HLN), comparing squared-error losses of
XGBoost (lags only) versus XGBoost (lags + meteorology) at physical horizons
$h\in\{1,6,12,24\}$.

Because forecasts are issued once per day (24-h origin spacing), the loss-differential
sequence advances in daily steps rather than in physical hours.  We therefore define
$h_{\text{DM}}=\lceil h/24\rceil$ as the horizon expressed in loss-differential steps.
For $h\in\{1,6,12,24\}$, $h_{\text{DM}}=1$ for all tests, so the mechanically induced
overlap bandwidth is $q_{\text{overlap}}=\max(0,h_{\text{DM}}-1)=0$.  This
overlap-based choice removes the \emph{mechanically required} positive-lag covariance
from overlapping multi-step forecast errors, but it does not imply that loss
differentials are independent or that residual dependence cannot exist.

To address potential positive-lag dependence not induced mechanically by overlap, we
also report a fixed automatic Newey--West sensitivity analysis \citep{NeweyWest1987}
using the bandwidth rule $q_{\text{auto}}=\lfloor 4\,(n/100)^{2/9}\rfloor$.

The primary inferential family comprises the 36 planned site--horizon comparisons
(9 sites $\times$ 4 horizons), controlled with global Bonferroni correction.  As a
within-station sensitivity analysis addressing a narrower inferential scope, we also
apply Benjamini--Hochberg false-discovery-rate control separately within each station
(4 horizons per station).

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\subsection{Madrid---substantial descriptive horizon extension}

Madrid offers the clearest visual contrast between the two XGBoost information sets.
Figure~\ref{fig:madrid_skill} shows that the lags-only model is persistence-superior only
over a limited portion of the 24-h range, whereas the meteorology-augmented model
maintains positive persistence-relative skill further into the day.  Summarising this
pattern with the primary strict descriptor, the longest-contiguous positive-skill run
increases from $H^*_{\text{strict}}=9$~h (lags only) to $H^*_{\text{strict}}=17$~h (lags +
meteorology), a strict extension of +8~h.  The relaxed descriptor highlights a more
modest change at the tail: $H^*_{\text{relax}}$ increases from 15 to 17~h (+2~h).  Reporting
both values is useful because they summarise different aspects of the same skill
profile---continuity versus the last isolated horizon with $S(h)>0$---and, in turn,
frame how compelling the descriptive horizon gain appears before any formal attribution.

\subsection{Cross-site heterogeneity and limited observable headroom}

The Irish network presents a different descriptive geometry.  In many panels of
Figure~\ref{fig:ireland_skill}, persistence-relative skill remains positive for much of
$h=1,\ldots,24$ under the lags-only specification, so a strict horizon descriptor often
reaches the design boundary even before meteorology is added.  Consistent with this
limited headroom, the mean strict horizon increases from 21.875 to 22.875~h (mean
increment +1.000~h), and five of eight stations already attain
$H^*_{\text{strict}}=24$~h with lags only.  Across both information sets, 11
station/condition cases reach $H^*_{\text{strict}}=24$~h, so several strict horizons are
right-censored by $H_{\max}=24$ rather than indicating a natural predictability limit.

Table~\ref{tab:ireland_hstar} provides the corresponding station-level horizon summaries.
Taken together, the Madrid and Ireland profiles suggest that ``hours gained'' is not a
stable descriptive object across sites, motivating a shift from visual horizon
extensions to inferential support for incremental meteorological information.

\subsection{Descriptive gains do not scale to family-wise global support}

The paired loss-differential comparisons provide that inferential layer.  Under the
primary dimensionally coherent overlap-based DM-HLN specification
($q_{\text{overlap}}=0$), all 36 planned station--horizon tests are valid
(Table~\ref{tab:dm_q0}).  While three comparisons yield raw $p<0.05$, none survives the
global Bonferroni correction across the 36-test family (0/36).  At a narrower scope,
station-wise Benjamini--Hochberg control retains three local signals (Birr at $h=1$,
Madrid at $h=12$, and Pearse St.\ Dublin at $h=6$).  These outcomes are compatible:
local signals can coexist with the absence of family-wise rejection, and 0/36 under the
global correction should not be interpreted as equivalence or proof of no effect.

\subsection{Global conclusion remains unchanged under fixed automatic Newey--West sensitivity}

The overlap-based choice $q_{\text{overlap}}=0$ addresses dependence mechanically induced
by forecast overlap under 24-h origin spacing, but it does not preclude residual
positive-lag dependence in the loss differentials.  Table~\ref{tab:dm_auto_nw} therefore
reports a fixed automatic Newey--West sensitivity.  The global picture is unchanged:
again, no comparison survives global Bonferroni correction (0/36).  Three within-station
Benjamini--Hochberg signals remain, but their identities are not identical.  Birr at
$h=1$ and Pearse St.\ Dublin at $h=6$ are retained under both specifications; Madrid at
$h=12$ appears only under the overlap-based specification, whereas Pearse St.\ Dublin at
$h=12$ appears under the automatic Newey--West sensitivity.

The evidence thus separates cleanly by scale: the family-wise global conclusion is
stable across the two audited dependence specifications, while the limited local signals
are more sensitive to that choice.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The descriptive evidence initially points in different directions depending on the
site.  In Madrid, adding meteorological observations reshapes the horizon-wise skill
profile enough to extend the strict positive-skill run from 9 to 17~h, a change that is
hard to ignore when viewed against persistence.  Yet the relaxed summary moves from 15
to 17~h, reminding us that even within a single site the apparent magnitude of a
``horizon extension'' depends on which descriptive question $H^*$ is asked to answer.

The Irish network adds a second layer of qualification.  Many lags-only skill curves
remain positive throughout most of the evaluated range, so the strict descriptor often
reaches $H_{\max}=24$ before meteorology is introduced.  The mean strict horizon still
increases, but only from 21.875 to 22.875~h (+1.000~h), and with five of eight lags-only
cases already at the boundary (11 station/condition cases at $H^*_{\text{strict}}=24$ in
total).  In this setting, small observed changes in $H^*$ are consistent with limited
observable headroom rather than with a definitive absence of incremental information.

Once the comparison is moved from descriptive summaries to paired loss-differential
inference, the scale of the evidential claim becomes explicit.  Under the primary
corrected DM-HLN specification ($q_{\text{overlap}}=0$), none of the 36 planned tests
survives global Bonferroni correction, even though a small number of within-station
Benjamini--Hochberg signals remain.  The absence of family-wise rejection should not be
read as equivalence; rather, it bounds what can be supported as a multi-site,
multi-horizon attribution of the descriptive gains to the added meteorological
information.

The dependence specification is the most immediate statistical pressure point for this
conclusion.  Because $q_{\text{overlap}}=0$ removes only dependence mechanically induced
by overlapping multi-step forecast errors, residual serial dependence in the
loss-differential sequence remains a plausible concern.  The fixed automatic
Newey--West sensitivity addresses that concern without tuning to outcomes.  It leaves
the family-wise global result unchanged (again 0/36 after global Bonferroni), while the
identity of the limited local signals shifts across specifications, tempering any
strong claim of local stability.

The evaluation geometry also carries a methodological lesson: when forecast origins are
spaced differently from physical horizons, DM-HLN/HAC parameters must be expressed in
loss-differential sequence steps rather than transferred mechanically from hours.

Within these bounds, the study supports a restrained interpretation.  Meteorological
covariates are origin-time observations rather than future NWP forecasts; the 24-h
horizon limit censors $H^*$ for several stations; and the evidence is specific to the
nine sites, the January--July 2023 evaluation window, and the evaluated XGBoost design.
The automatic Newey--West analysis is a fixed sensitivity rather than an exhaustive
dependence model, and descriptive improvements do not provide causal attribution.
Taken together, the results argue for keeping descriptive horizon extension, local
inferential signals, and family-wise global evidence as distinct levels of support
when interpreting incremental information in environmental forecasting.

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The evidence in this nine-site \PMten{} study is easiest to read when descriptive
horizon summaries and inferential attribution are kept separate.  Descriptively, the
Madrid skill profile changes markedly when meteorological observations are added,
extending the strict longest-contiguous positive-skill horizon from 9 to 17~h (with a
smaller relaxed change from 15 to 17~h), while the Irish network exhibits heterogeneous
and frequently boundary-limited behaviour within $H_{\max}=24$.

When the same lags-only versus lags+meteorology comparison is assessed through the 36
planned, dimensionally corrected DM-HLN tests, no comparison survives global Bonferroni
correction under either the overlap-based specification ($q_{\text{overlap}}=0$) or the
fixed automatic Newey--West sensitivity.  The global conclusion is therefore stable to
this bounded dependence treatment, even though the identities of the limited local BH
signals are not.

For multi-horizon environmental forecasting studies, the implication is bounded: a
compelling extension of persistence-relative skill can be descriptively substantial,
yet still fall short of supporting a family-wise global attribution to incremental
meteorological predictability under the planned inferential family.
